COMPLETE GUIDE TO MAIZE FARMING IN AFRICA

1. MAIZE VARIETIES FOR AFRICA
- Open Pollinated Varieties (OPV): Can save seeds, suit small farms
  * Local varieties: Adapted to climate, cheap
  * Improved OPV: Higher yields, disease resistant
- Hybrid Seeds: Better yields but must buy new each season
  * Single cross hybrids: 8-10 tons/hectare
  * Double cross hybrids: 6-8 tons/hectare
- Disease resistant varieties: Important in wet regions
  * Northern leaf blight resistant
  * Grey leaf spot resistant

2. LAND PREPARATION FOR MAIZE
- Soil Requirements:
  * pH 6.0-7.0 (test your soil)
  * Well-drained loamy soil best
  * Avoid waterlogged areas
- Clearing: Remove weeds, rocks, plant residue
- Plowing: Deep plowing (20-30cm) 4-6 weeks before planting
- Harrowing: Break soil clumps, create fine seedbed
- Ridging: Create ridges 75-80cm apart for water management

3. MAIZE PLANTING
- Timing: Plant at start of rains (March-April in East Africa)
- Seed rate: 25kg per hectare
- Spacing: 75cm between rows, 25cm between plants
- Depth: Plant 4-5cm deep
- Seed treatment: Treat with fungicide to prevent rot
- Population: About 53,000 plants per hectare

4. FERTILIZER FOR MAIZE
- Basal fertilizer (at planting):
  * DAP (18-46-0): 250kg/hectare
  * Or use NPK 20-20-0: 300kg/hectare
- Top dressing (6-8 weeks after planting):
  * Urea (46-0-0): 200-250kg/hectare
  * Apply when plants are knee-high
- Organic: Compost 5-10 tons/hectare
- Manure: Animal manure 10-15 tons/hectare

5. WEEDING IN MAIZE
- First weeding: 3-4 weeks after planting (knee-high)
- Second weeding: 6-8 weeks after planting (waist-high)
- Use hoe to remove weeds without damaging plants
- Remove manually between plants
- Mulch to reduce weed growth

6. IRRIGATION FOR MAIZE (If no rain)
- Water needs: 500-800mm total
- Critical periods for water:
  * Germination: 1st week
  * Flowering: 6-8 weeks
  * Grain filling: 8-12 weeks
- Irrigation methods:
  * Flood: Traditional, needs water channels
  * Drip: Water-efficient, 50% less water
  * Sprinkler: Flexible, needs pressure
- Frequency: Every 7-10 days, 25-40mm per irrigation

7. PEST MANAGEMENT IN MAIZE
- Common pests:
  * Fall armyworm: Eat leaves and corn
    - Control: Neem oil spray, hand picking
  * Stalk borers: Bore into stalks
    - Control: Pheromone traps, crop rotation
  * Grasshoppers: Eat leaves
    - Control: Early planting, bird perches
- Monitoring: Check plants weekly
- Biological: Use natural enemies (ladybugs, wasps)
- Chemical: Use only if pest pressure high

8. DISEASE MANAGEMENT
- Common diseases:
  * Grey leaf spot: Brown spots on leaves
    - Control: Remove infected leaves, crop rotation
  * Northern corn leaf blight: Long lesions on leaves
    - Control: Use resistant varieties
  * Maize streak virus: Yellow streaks, wilting
    - Control: Plant early, use resistant seeds
- Prevention: Crop rotation, resistant varieties, clean seed

9. HARVESTING MAIZE
- Timing: 120-150 days after planting
- Signs of maturity:
  * Kernels hard and dented
  * Husks brown and dry
  * Stalk brown and brittle
- Method: Snap cob from stalk by hand
- Drying: Dry in sun for 2-3 weeks (12% moisture for storage)
- Storage: Keep in cool, dry place, use hermetic containers

10. MAIZE YIELD IMPROVEMENT TIPS
- Use certified seeds (15-20% higher yield)
- Plant on time with rains (avoid delays)
- Apply full fertilizer dose (can increase yield 2-3x)
- Weed properly (reduces competition)
- Manage pests early (prevent damage)
- Harvest when mature (avoid losses)
- Expected yield: 3-4 tons/hectare (good farming)
                  5-6 tons/hectare (very good farming)
                  8-10 tons/hectare (excellent farming with irrigation + fertilizer)

11. MAIZE POST-HARVEST
- Shelling: Remove kernels from cob
- Sorting: Remove damaged/discolored kernels
- Drying: Dry to 12% moisture (can test with bite)
- Storage: Use airtight containers with desiccants
- Pest control in storage: Use neem leaves or ash
- Value addition: Make maize flour, animal feed, ethanol

12. COMMON MAIZE FARMING PROBLEMS & SOLUTIONS
Problem: Poor germination
- Cause: Seeds planted too deep, soil too wet/dry, old seeds
- Solution: Plant 4-5cm deep, ensure moist soil, use fresh seeds

Problem: Stunted growth
- Cause: Low nitrogen, poor soil, pests, drought
- Solution: Apply top dressing with urea, improve soil, irrigate

Problem: No ear development
- Cause: Water stress during flowering, poor nutrition, hot wind
- Solution: Irrigate during flowering, apply fertilizer, provide shade

Problem: Cob rot
- Cause: High humidity, poor drainage, fungal disease
- Solution: Improve drainage, remove infected plants, use fungicide

13. MAIZE FARMING ECONOMICS
- Cost per hectare:
  * Seeds: $20-30
  * Fertilizer: $60-80
  * Labor: $50-100
  * Pesticides: $20-30
  * Irrigation (if needed): $30-50
  * Total: $180-290 per hectare

- Income per hectare (average 3.5 tons):
  * Sell at $200/ton = $700 revenue
  * Profit: $700 - $250 = $450 per hectare

14. MAIZE FARMING FOR SMALL FARMERS
- Start small: 0.5-1 hectare
- Use family labor
- Save seeds from best plants
- Share equipment with neighbors
- Join farmer groups for inputs
- Sell directly to consumers (better prices)
